# Archive Coverage

The Digital Anatolian Heritage Archive separates **coverage** from **research depth**.

A place can belong to the archive before it has received a full interpretive research record. This distinction allows the project to build broad geographic coverage without presenting unverified discovery data as scholarship.

## Operational geographic scope

The registry indexes archaeological and historical places within modern Türkiye, centred on Anatolia while retaining explicit historical-region labels such as Thrace, Cilicia, Upper Mesopotamia, the Troad, Lycia, Phrygia and Pontus where appropriate.

The project does not force every place within modern Türkiye into a narrowly defined geographical use of the term “Anatolia”. Modern administrative location and historical-cultural geography are stored separately.

## Two archive layers

### Registry

The registry is the broad coverage layer. It is intended to include sites discovered through national lists, authority datasets, heritage registers and archaeological gazetteers.

Registry entries may contain only a canonical name, aliases, historical region and approximate modern location at first. Missing or uncertain information remains explicitly missing or uncertain.

### Sourced research records

Research records are the deeper editorial layer represented by the initial eight-site corpus. They require structured chronology, claim-level provenance, bibliography, media rights, interpretive caution and a public-facing research page.

A registry entry must not be described as a sourced research record until it meets that standard.

## Research status

Each registry entry carries one of four statuses:

- `indexed` — discovered in a seed catalogue; identity and location may still require checking.
- `location-verified` — modern location and geographic identity have been checked against a stronger source.
- `authority-linked` — linked to one or more stable identifiers such as Wikidata, UNESCO or Pleiades.
- `sourced-record` — represented by a full DAHA research record with scholarly/institutional provenance.

Status is cumulative in meaning but stored as one current state.

## Seed catalogues are discovery tools, not evidence

The Turkish Wikipedia page “Türkiye'deki antik kentler listesi” is useful because it aggregates a very large number of named sites, aliases, historical regions and modern locations in one place. It is treated as a **discovery seed only**.

Information imported from that list does not support archaeological interpretation, chronology or historical claims in DAHA. Entries are expected to be reconciled against stronger sources over time, including:

1. excavation publications and project databases;
2. Türkiye Ministry of Culture and Tourism and related official heritage resources;
3. UNESCO documentation where applicable;
4. institutional archaeological databases and gazetteers;
5. authority identifiers such as Wikidata and Pleiades.

The seed list contains unresolved locations, inconsistent dating, duplicate or variant entries and places assigned to historical regions outside a strict geographical definition of Anatolia. Those conditions are preserved as data-quality issues rather than silently normalised.

## Coverage does not mean completeness of knowledge

The archive may eventually achieve near-complete registry coverage for a defined source set while only a subset of entries have full research records. Public interfaces should therefore distinguish “indexed in the archive” from “researched by DAHA”.

This distinction is central to the project: breadth should not create false certainty.
